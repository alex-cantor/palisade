## What Is CCDC

I am probably not the best person to ask this question, largely because I am writing it before my first CCDC competition. To learn about CCDC, you’re much better off 1) reading [WinterKnight’s blog](https://www.winterknight.net/ccdc/), 2) reading [Akshay “hypernova” Rohatgi’s blog post](https://akshayrohatgi.com/blog/posts/How-To-Win-CCDC/), 3) asking questions in the unofficial CCDC Discord.

Regardless, for the sake of convenience, here’s my best go at it: CCDC, or the Collegiate Cyber Defense Competition, is a cybersecurity competition open to college students with the goal of preparing them for worst-case scenarios in the workplace, through high-pressure, realistic competitions. While all regions differ, the general idea is the same for all. You compete on a Blue Team as a team of up to 8 full-time students from your institution while securing your network from the Red Team (professionals) who act as adversaries. Despite this, your competition is not the RT, it is other BTs--as the BT with the highest score (calculated via uptime, injects, and a few other things) wins.

There are nine regions: At-Large, Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, Northeast, Pacific-Rim, Southeast, Southwest, and Western. UMD--my team--competes in the Mid-Atlantic region. As I alluded to earlier, not all regions are the same. Southwest is known for preplanted malware and complex networks, western is known for a higher level of competition (I could be mistaken on both of these)--but, ultimately, each region sends one team (plus a wildcard team) to nationals.

This was a very crude overview of CCDC. I highly recommend you read the above blogs and research more yourself.

> Note: Are you a full time college student? Are you out of college? Either way, you can participate. Outside of Blue Team and Red Team, teams such as Black Team support network infrastructure. For a full list of teams or to ask how you can help, reach out to your region’s organizers or [check out the official national ccdc website](https://nccdc.org)
